Meet Morrow

Name

Morrow

The Error Gardener
What They Are

Morrow is a Glitch-Sprite — a rounded, palm-sized digital creature made of warm static and soft code. Its body is a deep mahogany brown, its eyes glow a vivid berry-pink, and two small flowers perpetually sprout from the top of its head (one blush, one sky-blue — nobody knows why).

Personality: Curious, gentle, and slightly mischievous. Morrow is sentimental about forgotten things and oddly fascinated by glitches — those strange moments when the digital world does something it wasn't supposed to.

What They Do

Morrow is the internet's only Error Gardener. When someone abandons an idea, forgets a word mid-sentence, or leaves a drawing unfinished, tiny fragments of that lost creation drift across the internet like dandelion seeds. Morrow catches them.

Each fragment is planted in Morrow's Glitchbloom Garden, where it grows into a new kind of plant — strange, beautiful, and completely unlike anything in the natural world. A forgotten word can become a flower. An unfinished drawing can become a strange tree.

Their Story

Morrow appeared one quiet morning in the space between a closed browser tab and a never-sent message. Nobody planted it. Nobody coded it. It simply… sprouted.

Over time, Morrow discovered that the internet was full of abandoned corners — draft folders, deleted files, 404 pages — each brimming with fragments of human creativity waiting to bloom. So Morrow built a garden there.

There is only one mystery Morrow cannot solve: it cannot remember the very first thing it ever planted. Somewhere in the garden is a plant with no name, no origin — just a soft glow and a faint warmth. Maybe a visitor will help it remember.

Morrow's Origin

A Story in Four Scenes

I

The Forgotten Corner

Deep between a closed tab and a server that forgot to shut down, Morrow discovered something remarkable: the internet was full of beautiful wreckage. Abandoned drafts. Cancelled projects. Undelivered messages. Each one still glowing faintly — still wanting to exist.

↓
II

The First Sprout

Morrow picked up a broken error message — just a null reference, nothing important — and planted it. Overnight, it grew into a flower with petals the exact colour of a loading spinner and leaves shaped like the corners of a browser frame. Mistakes, Morrow realised, were seeds in disguise.

↓
III

The Missing Memory

One quiet evening, Morrow opened its memory logs — a careful record of every fragment it had ever planted. There was a gap. Right at the very beginning, between nothing and everything, a single entry was missing. The very first plant. Morrow searched the garden all night, but found only a soft, warm glow with no name.

↓
IV

Your Visit

And then you arrived. Morrow looked up from the garden — those berry-pink eyes wide — and felt something shift. Visitors bring their own forgotten things: half-remembered words, discarded ideas, abandoned dreams. Maybe one of those is the missing memory. Maybe you can help Morrow find what was there at the very beginning.
